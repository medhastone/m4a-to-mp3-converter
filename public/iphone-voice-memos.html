Why Voice Memos Require Specific Codecs

Apple natively records voice memos using the Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) format wrapped in an .m4a container. While highly efficient for mobile storage, AAC isn't universally supported by older transcription software, podcast hosting platforms, or Windows editing suites.

Human speech primarily occupies the frequency range of 85Hz to 255Hz. Standard music converters often apply wide-band compression that introduces background artifacts. By using a 192 kbps Mono preset, this transcoder ensures that bandwidth is exclusively dedicated to dialogue clarity, avoiding the phasing issues common in stereo upmixing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are iPhone voice memos stored?

By default, iPhone voice memos are stored within the Voice Memos app. You can export them as .m4a files by tapping the three dots next to a recording and selecting 'Save to Files' or 'AirDrop'.

Can I convert voice memos directly inside mobile Safari?

Yes! Our converter runs 100% in your browser using Web Audio API. You can select the file directly from your iCloud Drive or 'Files' app via Safari.

Is there a recording duration limit?

Since the conversion happens locally on your device's RAM without server uploads, the limit depends on your device's memory. Most modern iPhones and desktops can handle 1-2 hour lectures easily.
